# Video Representation: US–China Competition in Africa (2026–2036)

## Working title
**"Africa at the Center: The Next Decade of US–China Competition"**

## Format
- Length: 7–9 minutes
- Style: explainer + geopolitical documentary
- Audience: general public, students, policy watchers

---

## Scene-by-scene storyboard + narration script

### Scene 1 (0:00–0:45) — Opening: The New Strategic Center
**Visuals**
- Satellite view of Earth zooming into Africa.
- Overlay: shipping lanes, undersea cables, ports, rare mineral locations.
- Split-screen of Washington and Beijing policy rooms.

**Narration**
> "The world’s strategic center is shifting. Across Africa, a quiet contest is accelerating—between the United States and China. It is not just about influence. It is about supply chains, energy, digital infrastructure, security, and the rules of the next global economy."

**On-screen text**
- "Population by 2050: ~1 in 4 people globally will be African"
- "Critical minerals, trade routes, data corridors"

---

### Scene 2 (0:45–2:00) — What Is at Stake
**Visuals**
- Lithium, cobalt, copper, manganese mine footage.
- EV battery assembly lines in Asia, Europe, and North America.
- Power grids, new rail corridors, port cranes, fintech use in African cities.

**Narration**
> "Africa sits on resources central to 21st-century industry: battery minerals, natural gas, solar potential, and strategic geography connecting Atlantic and Indo-Pacific routes. Whoever helps finance extraction, processing, logistics, and digital systems gains long-term leverage."

**Key stake cards (animated)**
1. **Industrial future** — control of mineral processing and battery value chains.
2. **Energy transition** — renewables, gas, grid investment, carbon markets.
3. **Digital sovereignty** — 5G, cloud, AI governance, cybersecurity standards.
4. **Security architecture** — counterterrorism, maritime security, peace operations.
5. **Diplomatic alignment** — voting blocs in UN and multilateral forums.

---

### Scene 3 (2:00–3:20) — China’s Position
**Visuals**
- Belt and Road infrastructure montage: roads, rail, ports, industrial parks.
- Chinese construction firms and financing announcements.
- Minerals-to-manufacturing map arrows.

**Narration**
> "China entered early, moved fast, and built at scale. Its model often combines state-backed finance, construction delivery, and long-term commodity access. In many countries, Chinese firms are embedded in roads, power, telecom, and mining ecosystems."

**On-screen balance note**
- Benefits: rapid infrastructure delivery, visible assets.
- Risks: debt distress in some contexts, project transparency concerns, local jobs/transfer disputes.

---

### Scene 4 (3:20–4:30) — The US Approach
**Visuals**
- US-Africa business summits, DFC-backed projects, clean-energy and health partnerships.
- Clips of startups, private equity, and local entrepreneurship ecosystems.

**Narration**
> "Washington is trying a different playbook: private capital mobilization, selective strategic finance, security cooperation, and partnerships framed around transparency and governance. The challenge is speed. African governments often compare execution timelines, not speeches."

**On-screen balance note**
- Strengths: innovation ecosystem, education ties, capital markets, security partnerships.
- Weaknesses: slower delivery, fragmented coordination, policy continuity concerns.

---

### Scene 5 (4:30–5:45) — African Agency: Not a New Cold War
**Visuals**
- African Union sessions, regional blocs, heads of state meeting multiple partners.
- Flags of Gulf states, EU, India, Turkey, and others joining the map.

**Narration**
> "This is not a two-player board. African governments are not passive pieces—they negotiate, hedge, and diversify. Many seek ‘multi-alignment’: Chinese infrastructure, Western finance, Gulf logistics, and regional industrial policy at the same time."

**On-screen text**
- "Core objective for African states: maximize development options, avoid dependency traps."

---

### Scene 6 (5:45–7:10) — Three Plausible Futures (2026–2036)

#### Scenario A: **Competitive Coexistence** (most likely)
**Visuals:** Dual-track projects; countries sourcing from both powers.

**Narration:**
> "Most likely, competition intensifies but remains bounded. African states continue blending partners by sector. China keeps infrastructure and mineral depth; the US gains ground in digital services, finance, health, and high-standard energy projects."

#### Scenario B: **Bloc Fragmentation**
**Visuals:** Red/blue map hardening; sanctions overlays; supply chains splitting.

**Narration:**
> "If US–China tensions escalate globally, pressure to choose sides could increase. That would raise costs, delay projects, and constrain African policy autonomy."

#### Scenario C: **African Industrial Leap**
**Visuals:** Local refining, battery precursor plants, transmission corridors, regional trade hubs.

**Narration:**
> "If governance, power reliability, and regional integration improve, Africa captures more value onshore—moving from raw exports toward refining, manufacturing, and digital services. External competition then becomes a bargaining advantage, not a trap."

---

### Scene 7 (7:10–8:15) — Indicators to Watch
**Visual dashboard animation**
1. Share of minerals processed in Africa vs exported raw.
2. Debt restructuring terms and transparency quality.
3. Grid reliability and cross-border power trade growth.
4. Data center and cloud region expansion.
5. Local job creation and technology transfer clauses.
6. Security spillovers around ports, corridors, and maritime chokepoints.

**Narration**
> "The next decade will be decided less by summit speeches, and more by these measurable outcomes: who builds, who trains, who transfers technology, and who helps African economies move up the value chain."

---

### Scene 8 (8:15–8:45) — Closing
**Visuals**
- Young workforce in manufacturing, coding labs, logistics hubs.
- Final map transitions from ‘competition’ to ‘co-development pathways’.

**Narration**
> "What is at stake is bigger than a rivalry. It is whether Africa becomes the arena of great-power extraction—or a decisive architect of the 21st-century economy. The choices made now will shape global power, prosperity, and stability for decades."

---
